FLOODVIEW

Geo-tagged flood ground truthing. Every photo records where you stand, where the camera looks, its ground footprint, and what the water is doing.

Needs: Camera · GPS · Compass

⚠ Stay safe. Never wade into moving water: 15 cm of fast water can knock an adult over, and 30 cm can carry a car away. Tag from dry ground; the crosshair records the spot you aim at, not just where you stand.
How it works: aim at the scene and tap FLOODED, WATERLOGGED or DRY. One tap saves the photo with its class and your tags (depth, flow, trend, what's flooded). Turn on Edge and put the crosshair on the waterline to trace the flood boundary.

After the flood? Choose After flood when you start a survey to record flood marks, damage and places that stayed dry.

When you're done, export a ZIP from the Data tab and open it in the Explorer.

🌊 Purpose

Field ground truth for flood maps, for example Sentinel-1 SAR or optical flood extents. Each capture stores the observer position, the camera look direction, the ground footprint and the point under the crosshair, plus a class (flooded, waterlogged or dry), water depth, flow, level trend, what is flooded and optional water-edge markers.

Open sourceWorks offlineGeoJSON · CSVSibling of CropView

🧭 Field routine

  1. Wait for a GPS fix and calibrate the compass (figure-8; tap the compass).
  2. Set the tags that apply: depth, flow, trend, what's flooded.
  3. Aim and tap FLOODED, WATERLOGGED or DRY.
  4. For the boundary, switch on Edge and put the crosshair on the waterline.
  5. Export a ZIP and open it in the Explorer.

Open ZIPs in the FloodView Explorer to map, review and correct the labels. It also reads CropView ZIPs.

⚠️ Disclaimer

Position, heading, tilt, footprint and crosshair point depend on the phone's sensors and can be off by several metres or degrees. Depth on the body scale is an estimate. Validate before using for decisions, and never put yourself at risk to collect a point.
